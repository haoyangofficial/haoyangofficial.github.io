**Abstract**:&emsp; Transmitting high-resolution remote sensing imagery remains challenging due to bandwidth limitations and high communication overhead, despite its importance for environmental observation, urban analysis, and land-cover mapping. Traditional approaches downlink entire pixel maps, resulting in significant redundancy and inefficient resource usage. This work proposes Text-RSR, a text-guided reconstruction framework that enables semantic-efficient image transmission within a compact remote sensing system. Instead of sending full-resolution data, our method transmits a lightweight low-resolution image accompanied by descriptive text tokens generated by an onboard vision-language encoder. These textual cues encapsulate key spatial and semantic content, compressing the transmitted data to 11.3\% of the original size on the Alsat-2B dataset. On the receiver side, a text-conditioned reconstruction network learns to fuse linguistic and visual modalities to recover high-resolution detail. Evaluations on Alsat-2B, UC Merced Land Use, and Aerial Image datasets yield PSNRs of 16.36 dB, 27.49 dB, and 27.41 dB, verifying that Text-RSR achieves effective and information-preserving transmission. 
All code and pretrained models will be released publicly.
